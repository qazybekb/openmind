# OpenMind Product Best Practices Research

**Research date:** September 4, 2026 (America/Los_Angeles)  
**Scope:** Repository commit `0a88676`, public website as served on the research date, and current primary/maintained sources. Application code was not changed.

Labels used below: **Established practice** is supported by standards, research, or mature documented systems; **Recommendation** is a proposed OpenMind decision; **Assumption** is unverified and should be tested with students.

## Executive assessment

OpenMind has a defensible advantage over a general chatbot: it can turn a student's own bCourses deadlines, grades, rubrics, modules, and readings into timely, course-specific help. The strongest initial product is therefore a **trustworthy academic command center and tutor**, not a universal personal assistant. Practice testing and distributed practice have unusually strong learning evidence, while summarization alone is relatively weak; an effective tutor should retrieve course evidence, ask the student to recall or apply it, provide feedback, and schedule later review ([Dunlosky et al.](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266)). A carefully designed college AI tutor has shown promising gains in one randomized physics study, but that result does not establish that arbitrary chatbot tutoring works across subjects ([Kestin et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40537565/)).

The repository is a credible prototype, not yet a reliable beta for students to depend on. The existing review's highest-impact claims are supported by the code: document text can influence tool authorization (`src/openmind/bot.py`, `src/openmind/llm.py`); model-produced LaTeX is executed with the user's filesystem permissions (`src/openmind/tools/studyguide.py`); background Gmail content can reach OpenRouter without an explicit opt-in (`src/openmind/heartbeat.py`); and retries/sync state can duplicate writes or preserve stale deadlines. The 22 passing tests are useful but concentrate on narrow unit/release contracts rather than realistic agent trajectories, integration failure sequences, timezone boundaries, or adversarial documents.

The immediate decision is not which agent framework to adopt. A small team can retain the current single Python orchestrator while introducing deterministic authorization, typed contracts, explicit consent, and durable SQLite state. Reliability and privacy promises should precede more integrations.

## Product goals and assumptions

**Recommended goal:** In under ten minutes, a Berkeley student can safely connect bCourses and answer: “What needs attention, why, and what should I do next?” They can then study from cited course material through retrieval practice, in terminal or Telegram, with predictable cost and no surprising writes or background disclosure.

**MVP boundary:** bCourses read access; deadline/grade/announcement views; cited assignment and course-material Q&A; guided practice; terminal first, with Telegram as an optional companion. Calendar export may remain an explicitly confirmed beta. Defer Gmail, Slack, Todoist, Obsidian writes, career advising, campus discovery, and arbitrary LaTeX generation until the core is measured and safe. This is a product-focus recommendation, not a claim that students do not value those features.

**Unverified assumptions:** students will install a Python CLI; Berkeley permits the intended personal-token usage; students trust a third-party model gateway with course records; Telegram is a desired daily surface; and deadline prioritization improves outcomes. Validate these before optimizing breadth.

## Findings by research area

### 1. Product focus and user needs

**Established practice.** AI should solve a distinct user need and disclose capability limits; mismatched mental models cause abandonment ([Google PAIR](https://pair.withgoogle.com/guidebook-v2/chapter/mental-models/)). Berkeley itself advises that AI output may be confidently wrong and should be cross-checked, while course-level AI rules vary ([Berkeley RTL](https://rtl.berkeley.edu/resources/ai-teaching-learning)).

**Repository finding.** The homepage and `README.md` lead with bCourses but quickly advertise 43 tools and 80+ prompts (`website/src/components/Features.astro`, `website/src/pages/use-cases.astro`). The unique value is buried among features a general chatbot or existing app already handles. Course advising is also presented despite the bundled catalog lacking live enrollment, prerequisites, or authoritative advising status.

**Recommendation.** Center three jobs: (1) a verified weekly/next-action view, (2) cited explanation of an assignment or course source, and (3) active study sessions with recall, feedback, and spaced follow-up. Run a 4–6 week opt-in pilot with 12–20 students across course types. Measure successful setup, weekly retained use, deadline accuracy, cited-answer acceptance/correction, completed practice sessions, and interviews about avoided work. Do not use raw message volume as the primary demand signal.

### 2. Onboarding and user experience

**Established practice.** Human-AI interfaces should state what the system can do, how well it does it, and support correction ([Microsoft HAI Guidelines](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/articles/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction-eighteen-best-practices-for-human-centered-ai-design/)). WCAG 2.2 AA requires reflow without lost functionality at 320 CSS pixels ([W3C WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/wcag/#reflow)).

**Repository finding.** Setup requires Python, a manually generated Canvas token, an OpenRouter account/key, and optionally multi-step OAuth/app creation (`docs/SETUP.md`). OpenRouter validation calls the public models endpoint, so invalid keys can pass (`src/openmind/setup_wizard.py`). Defaults and model claims conflict across `README.md`, `docs/SETUP.md`, `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`, and `src/openmind/llm.py`. The live Gmail guide says email reaches the LLM only on request, contrary to the heartbeat default. The prior review also reproduced mobile overflow in install examples. Session cost estimates are static and unsupported, even though OpenRouter returns exact per-request cost and token usage ([OpenRouter usage accounting](https://openrouter.ai/docs/cookbook/administration/usage-accounting)).

**Recommendation.** Make setup resumable and staged: install/self-check, bCourses connection, first useful answer, then optional Telegram/integrations. Validate the API key through OpenRouter's authenticated current-key endpoint ([API reference](https://openrouter.ai/docs/api/api-reference/api-keys/get-current-key)). Add `openmind doctor` with actionable categories (credential expired, service unavailable, dependency missing, permission denied) and identical semantic responses in both channels. Show current model, estimated maximum before an expensive run, exact cost afterward, month-to-date local totals, and how to set an OpenRouter key spending limit. Fix 320 px reflow, keyboard/focus behavior, contrast, and copy controls before acquisition work.

### 3. Agent architecture and reliability

**Established practice.** Prompt text is not an authorization boundary. OWASP recommends least privilege, schema validation, separation of untrusted content, and human approval for high-impact actions ([AI Agent Security](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/AI_Agent_Security_Cheat_Sheet.html); [Prompt Injection Prevention](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/LLM_Prompt_Injection_Prevention_Cheat_Sheet.html)). HTTP clients should not automatically retry non-idempotent operations unless they can prove they are safe ([RFC 9110](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#name-idempotent-methods)).

**Repository finding.** Tools are conditionally exposed, but authorization is keyword matching over recent user-role messages (`src/openmind/llm.py`); Telegram embeds extracted PDF text in that role (`src/openmind/bot.py`). “Core” tools include profile updates, reminders, web access, PDF fetch, and expensive study-guide generation without equivalent gates (`src/openmind/tools/__init__.py`). Schemas are passed to the model, then malformed values are loosely cast rather than fully validated. Streaming fallback restarts from original messages after completed tool calls, enabling duplicate writes.

**Recommendation.** Represent every turn as separate trusted fields: original user intent, attachments/untrusted content, and tool results. Assign tools a deterministic policy: local/read-only; external/read-only; reversible write; irreversible/sensitive write; expensive. Permit reads only within explicit integration scope, and require parameter-bound confirmation for every write or costly generation, with a clear preview and approve/edit/cancel. Validate arguments and results against strict schemas (`additionalProperties: false`, bounds, formats) using [JSON Schema 2020-12](https://json-schema.org/draft/2020-12). Persist a run and each tool-call result before continuing; use idempotency keys or lookup-before-create. A separate agent framework is optional, not a prerequisite.

### 4. Privacy and local execution

**Established practice.** “Local” should describe data flow precisely. Zotero, for example, is usable with local storage and no account, while sync is an explicit optional service ([Zotero privacy](https://www.zotero.org/support/privacy)). OpenRouter says its own prompt logging is opt-in, but upstream endpoint retention varies; requests can enforce zero-data-retention endpoints ([OpenRouter ZDR](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/zdr)). Google recommends platform secure storage such as macOS Keychain and revocation/deletion when tokens are no longer needed ([OAuth best practices](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/resources/best-practices)). FERPA obligations depend on who operates the product and under what relationship; do not claim compliance without counsel ([US Department of Education](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/privacy-and-data-sharing)).

**Repository finding.** Long-lived tokens share plaintext JSON, albeit mode `0600` (`src/openmind/config.py`); profile fields are sent on every model turn (`docs/PRIVACY.md`). The default provider-routing request does not require ZDR. Background Gmail inference is not consistently opt-in. Deletion guidance is an all-or-nothing shell command and does not revoke remote credentials.

**Recommendation.** Market “local orchestration” unless local inference is actually offered. Before first model call, show a concise data-flow receipt: categories sent, OpenRouter/upstream provider, retention routing, approximate cost, and background status. Default background processing off and ZDR on, with explicit tradeoff that compatible model/provider choices may shrink. Move secrets to OS keyring where available, retaining a documented file fallback. Add inspect/export/delete-by-category and disconnect/revoke instructions. Minimize prompts: send only relevant profile/course fields and never silently forward Gmail/Slack bodies.

PDFs and fetched documents are both parser inputs and indirect-prompt inputs. Enforce byte/page/text limits, magic-type checks, timeouts, and no tool authorization from document text ([OWASP File Upload](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/File_Upload_Cheat_Sheet.html)). Stop compiling model-supplied LaTeX directly. Prefer an escaped, fixed HTML/Markdown template and trusted PDF renderer; if TeX is retained, allowlist a constrained document representation and compile inside a separately tested OS/container sandbox. A temporary directory and disabled shell escape are insufficient because common TeX configurations allow broad reads (`openin_any = a` in the documented [TeX configuration](https://sources.debian.org/src/texlive-base/2022.20230122-3/texmf-dist/web2c/texmf.cnf)).

### 5. Integration and background-job correctness

**Established practice.** Calendar timestamps require explicit offsets/timezones, and Google supports private extended properties for stable app identifiers and patch updates ([Events](https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/v3/reference/events); [extended properties](https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/guides/extended-properties)). Todoist's Sync API uses command UUIDs to prevent repeat execution ([Todoist API](https://developer.todoist.com/api/v1/)). APIs may return `Retry-After`; retries need bounded exponential backoff and jitter ([RFC 9110](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#field.retry-after)).

**Repository finding.** Heartbeat state is several JSON files; a PID file is not robust process ownership. UTC date strings are sliced before Berkeley conversion, synced items are never updated, and destination IDs are not saved (`src/openmind/heartbeat.py`). Reminders share an hourly tick, while documentation implies timely delivery. Gmail/Calendar refresh tokens are refreshed, but failures often collapse into “not ready” without a first-class reauthorization state (`src/openmind/tools/gmail.py`, `calendar.py`). Offline operations have no durable queue/status.

**Recommendation.** Use one local SQLite database with transactional tables for jobs, runs, source revisions, destination IDs, attempts, next-attempt time, and delivery receipts. Normalize instants in UTC while preserving source timezone and render in `America/Los_Angeles` via Python `zoneinfo`; test DST and 11:59 p.m. boundaries. Implement reconcile semantics (create/update/delete or explicitly leave orphan), provider idempotency, rate-limit-aware retries, and a visible status/repair command. “Offline” should mean reads from clearly timestamped cached data and queued writes that require review before later delivery, not silent failure.

### 6. Quality and operations

**Established practice.** Agent evals should be task-specific and include full traces, not only final prose; real corrections should become regression cases ([OpenAI eval loop](https://openai.com/index/building-self-improving-tax-agents-with-codex/)). Privacy-conscious diagnostics should redact tokens, personal data, and location by default, as Home Assistant requires of integration diagnostics ([Home Assistant diagnostics](https://developers.home-assistant.io/docs/core/integration/diagnostics/)). Reproducible application installs benefit from locked dependencies; PyPA now standardizes `pylock.toml` and recommends trusted publishing ([PyPA](https://packaging.python.org/en/latest/specifications/pylock-toml/)).

**Repository finding.** CI runs only Python 3.11 despite declared 3.11–3.13 support, loose lower-bound dependencies, and no lock for an end-user application (`pyproject.toml`, `.github/workflows/ci.yml`). Tests do not cover actual HTTP contracts, multi-turn authorization, partial writes, or website accessibility. Academic responses have no enforced source locator/provenance contract.

**Recommendation.** Build a small, de-identified eval set around the three MVP jobs. Score deadline/grade exactness, citation existence and source support, tool selection/denial, write confirmation, recovery, latency, and cost. For academic answers, show bCourses source title plus assignment/module/file locator and distinguish extracted fact, inference, and general-model knowledge. Add contract tests with recorded synthetic responses; property tests for dates/IDs; adversarial PDFs/web/email; and end-to-end terminal/Telegram parity. Define initial budgets, then adjust from pilot data: routine p95 response under 10 seconds, zero unconfirmed writes, zero duplicate sync writes, and a visible per-run cost ceiling. Keep local structured logs opt-in for export, redacted by default, with no message bodies.

### 7. Website messaging

Lead with the literal outcome: “Your bCourses deadlines and course materials, turned into a cited study plan.” Avoid “any LLM,” tool count, mascot personality, and unsupported cost/free-credit claims in the first viewport. Say “runs on your Mac; selected content is sent through OpenRouter to the chosen provider” beside the install action. Publish a capability matrix with read/write/background, data sent, cost class, maturity, and known limitations. This should be generated or tested against code-owned metadata to prevent the drift visible across `README.md`, `docs/SETUP.md`, `docs/PRIVACY.md`, and the live site.

## Comparable-product comparison

| Product/project | Documented design choice | Lesson that fits OpenMind | What not to copy |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Anki** | Uses an explicit scheduling algorithm (FSRS/SM-2), configurable retention, and review history rather than free-form “study” chat ([Anki FAQ](https://faqs.ankiweb.net/what-spaced-repetition-algorithm)). | Make recall events and review scheduling first-class data; let the LLM create/explain questions, not invent scheduling policy. | Anki's configuration depth is too much for MVP onboarding. |
| **Zotero** | Local library works without an account; sync is optional. Sync tracks pristine versions and prompts only for real field conflicts ([privacy](https://www.zotero.org/support/privacy); [sync design](https://www.zotero.org/support/dev/web_api/v3/syncing)). Citations retain structured links to sources. | Separate local use from cloud/model disclosure; retain source IDs/locators and reconcile versions. | Full collaborative conflict resolution is unnecessary until OpenMind supports bidirectional edits. |
| **Home Assistant** | Its integration quality scale requires connection tests, unique IDs, reauthentication, known limitations, diagnostics, and broad integration tests ([quality scale](https://developers.home-assistant.io/docs/core/integration-quality-scale/rules/)). | Adopt a lightweight checklist for each integration and redacted `doctor` output. | Do not reproduce its platform/plugin scope or 95% coverage rule mechanically. |
| **Open WebUI** | Tool creation is disabled for normal users and documented as root-equivalent; tools can be enabled per chat ([permissions](https://docs.openwebui.com/features/authentication-access/rbac/permissions/); [tools](https://docs.openwebui.com/features/extensibility/plugin/tools/)). | Make enabled capabilities visible per session and treat executable/extensible tools as a distinct trust tier. | Multi-user RBAC is unnecessary for a single-user local app. |
| **LangGraph** | Checkpointed steps, human interrupts, and idempotent side effects support resume after failure ([durable execution](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langgraph/functional-api)). | Persist run/tool state and approvals. | Adding the framework now may exceed the problem; implement the small required state machine first. |

## Suggested sequence: immediate, next, and later

**Immediate (before more testers):** resolve the four P0 classes; make Gmail background behavior opt-in; correct privacy/cost/local claims; fix mobile reflow; validate OpenRouter keys correctly; and add regression tests for every reproduced review finding.

**Next (private pilot):** narrow the surfaced MVP, add cited evidence objects, progressive setup and `doctor`, SQLite durable state, terminal/Telegram parity tests, actual cost display, and a de-identified eval/pilot scorecard. Ship to a small, consented Berkeley cohort and interview both retained and dropped users.

**Later (only after thresholds):** reintroduce integrations individually using the quality checklist; add adaptive spaced review; consider local-model support; package a friendlier macOS installer; add automatic update checks with signed/reproducible release metadata. Do not add a multi-agent architecture unless eval traces show the single orchestrator is the limiting factor.

## Open questions for our review

1. Is OpenMind an independent student tool, a research pilot, or intended for university adoption? Privacy/legal obligations and OAuth verification differ.
2. Which single outcome matters most: fewer missed deadlines, less planning time, better learning, or one daily interface?
3. Should any third-party content be sent to a model by default, or should every data category require opt-in?
4. Are write integrations essential to the value proposition, or can MVP export previews the student applies manually?
5. What course AI-policy signal can OpenMind reliably obtain, and should assignment-help features require the student to acknowledge it?
6. What pilot thresholds justify Telegram, Gmail/Slack, document generation, and advising as maintained features?
7. Is macOS the supported product platform? If so, can installation and Keychain integration become first-class rather than treating all OSes equally?

## Sources and research limitations

Primary sources are linked next to claims. The most influential were the [Berkeley teaching guidance](https://rtl.berkeley.edu/resources/ai-teaching-learning), [UC learning-data principles](https://rtl.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/general/uc_learning_data_principles_final03.05.2018.pdf), [OpenRouter privacy and routing documentation](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/privacy/data-collection), [Canvas API documentation](https://developerdocs.instructure.com/services/canvas), [Google OAuth guidance](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/resources/best-practices), [OAuth native-app BCP](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8252), [OWASP agent guidance](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/AI_Agent_Security_Cheat_Sheet.html), [W3C WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/wcag/), and the maintained comparable-product documentation cited above.

No personal credentials, private accounts, live student records, or third-party authenticated APIs were accessed. The public site returned HTTP 200 and still contained the Gmail “only when you ask” claim on the research date. Repository claims were checked statically and against `.context/project-website-review.md`; this work did not repeat every prior proof of concept. The local suite passed (`22 passed`) but is not evidence of production reliability. Cost/model availability and provider retention policies change frequently, so the product should fetch and display them rather than encode research-date values. FERPA, UC policy, Canvas terms, and OAuth verification implications require institutional/legal review; this document does not provide legal advice. Pilot sizes and success thresholds above are recommendations, not established benchmarks.
